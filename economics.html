Carrier economics: what we can write and what it earns

Phase 1 = existing admitted CA filing, opened for new business · Phase 2 = expansion filing · 2027 scenario model

Our own CA loss history says: only write the lower-wildfire-risk homes. On that basis, expanding HHIP into CA on our existing admitted paper gets us ~1,242 policies and $2.7M in 2027, at about break-even versus commission.

HHIP's historical CA book ran a 89% loss ratio in 2023–25 (63% excluding cat). A Hippo policy beats keeping the customer on 3P paper only below ~58%. The scored, lower-wildfire-band business written in 2024–25 ran 57%. Phase 1 opens CA new business on Jan 1 under our existing admitted filing; Phase 2 expands eligibility once a new rate/UW filing is approved (~9–12 months).

Key takeaways
Selection is the whole case.The whole CA book ran 89% in 2023–25. Lower wildfire bands ran 57% in 2024–25; the highest band ran 107%. Water, not fire, is the largest loss driver (47% of premium).
Renewals come first.35% of 2027 premium is re-shopping our own FAIR/E&S book, which costs almost nothing to acquire.
Expansion takes time.A new filing takes ~9–12 months to approve, or 18+ if an intervenor objects, so Phase 2 adds little before Q4 2027.
Our partner carriers are pulling back.Bamboo, Aegis and others bind 50–90% less per month than in Q1. That leaves room for us, and is also a warning about CA wildfire pricing.

Property

  • Roof age (40% over 20 yrs)
  • Rebuild cost limits
  • Vacancy and rentals (15%)

Regulatory

  • Rates fixed at the approved filing
  • Safer from Wildfires discounts
  • Post-fire non-renewal moratoriums
